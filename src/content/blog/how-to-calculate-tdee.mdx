## What Is TDEE?

TDEE stands for **Total Daily Energy Expenditure** — the total number of calories your body burns in a 24-hour period when you factor in your activity level.

It's made up of four components:

1. **BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)** — calories burned at complete rest (~60–75% of TDEE)
2. **NEAT (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis)** — walking, fidgeting, daily movement (~15–20%)
3. **EAT (Exercise Activity Thermogenesis)** — deliberate exercise (~5–10%)
4. **TEF (Thermic Effect of Food)** — digesting and processing food (~10%)

## Why TDEE Matters More Than Any Diet Rule

Every successful nutrition strategy — keto, paleo, intermittent fasting, calorie counting — works for one reason: **it puts you in a calorie deficit, maintenance, or surplus relative to your TDEE.**

If you know your TDEE, you can engineer the exact outcome you want:

| Goal | Target Calories |
|------|----------------|
| Lose fat | TDEE − 300 to 500 kcal/day |
| Maintain weight | TDEE (maintenance) |
| Build muscle | TDEE + 200 to 300 kcal/day |

## How to Calculate Your TDEE

The most accurate free method is the **Mifflin-St Jeor equation** × an activity multiplier.

**Step 1: Calculate BMR**

- Men: `(10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) − (5 × age) + 5`
- Women: `(10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) − (5 × age) − 161`

**Step 2: Multiply by activity level**

| Activity Level | Multiplier |
|----------------|-----------|
| Sedentary (desk job, no exercise) | × 1.2 |
| Lightly active (1–3 days/week) | × 1.375 |
| Moderately active (3–5 days/week) | × 1.55 |
| Very active (6–7 days/week) | × 1.725 |
| Athlete (physical job + training) | × 1.9 |

## Common Mistakes When Using TDEE

**Overestimating activity.** Most people pick "moderately active" when they should pick "lightly active." Be honest — gym sessions don't compensate for 8 hours of sitting.

**Ignoring NEAT.** Your unconscious daily movement can vary by 800+ calories between individuals. This is why two people with the same BMR can have very different TDEEs.

**Treating TDEE as fixed.** As you lose weight, your BMR drops. Recalculate every 4–6 weeks or when your weight loss stalls.

## The Bottom Line

Your TDEE is not a magic number — it's an **estimate** with roughly ±10–15% error. Use it as a starting point, then adjust based on real-world results after 2–3 weeks.
